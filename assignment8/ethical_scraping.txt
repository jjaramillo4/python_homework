Ethical Web Scraping - Wikipedia robots.txt analysis
Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/robots.txt

1. Which sections of the website are restricted for crawling?
   Key Disallow paths include:
   - /w/            (internal wiki infrastructure)
   - /api/          (API endpoints)
   - /wiki/Special: (special pages)
   - Editorial/administrative pages such as:
       /wiki/Wikipedia:Articles_for_deletion/
       /wiki/Wikipedia:Requests_for_adminship/
       /wiki/Wikipedia:Requests_for_arbitration/
       /wiki/Wikipedia:Copyright_problems
   These block search/edit infrastructure and keep administrative
   discussion pages out of search engine rankings.

2. Are there specific rules for certain user agents?
   Yes. Wikipedia names and fully blocks badly-behaved bots, for example:
   - MJ12bot     (spammed pages and ignored rate limits)
   - wget        (problematic in recursive mode)
   - grub-client, WebReaper, HTTrack, WebStripper (aggressive site-copying bots)
   It also includes Allow: exceptions, e.g. /w/api.php?action=mobileview&

3. Why do websites use robots.txt?
   robots.txt lets a site tell crawlers which areas they may and may not
   access. It protects server resources and keeps non-content or
   administrative pages out of automated crawls. Honoring it is the baseline
   of ethical scraping - it respects the site owner's stated wishes even
   though it is not legally enforced.
